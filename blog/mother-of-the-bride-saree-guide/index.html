Mother of the Bride Saree Guide: Comfort for a Long Wedding Day

26 November 2026·Mahavir Sarees, Rewari

Short answerThe best mother of the bride saree is one she can wear comfortably for 10 hours or more. Keep the heaviest pure silk for the phera, choose lighter sarees for other functions, pin the pleats, pallu and waist so the drape holds, and pick a rich colour that sits one or two shades away from the bride's outfit rather than matching it.

When a daughter's wedding is fixed, everyone talks about the bride's lehenga. Very few people plan the mother of the bride saree with the same care, and she is the one who is on her feet the longest. This mother of the bride saree guide from Mahavir Sarees, Rewari, is about one thing other guides skip: comfort across a 10-hour wedding day. We cover how heavy a saree should be, how to drape and pin it so it stays put, and which colours look good in photos next to the bride.

Her day is longer than anyone else's

On the wedding day, the bride's mother greets the baraat, looks after the guests, sits through the pheras, does the kanyadaan and stands at the vidaai. In winter that can run from late afternoon well past midnight. So the right question is not "which saree looks the grandest?" but "which saree will still feel easy at 1 AM?"

Here is how the day usually goes for her, and what the saree has to handle at each point.

Weight: the test to do in the shop

A saree that feels fine for five minutes in the mirror can pull at the shoulder by the third hour. Most of the weight sits in the pallu and border, so that is what to check.

  • Hold the pallu, not the fold. Ask the shopkeeper to open the pallu and lay it over your left shoulder. Stand with it for a minute. If you already feel it dragging, it will feel much heavier by midnight.
  • Look at where the zari is. A saree with zari spread across the body is heavier all over. One with zari mostly in the border and pallu feels lighter to wear, even if it looks just as rich.
  • Think about your back and knees. If sitting on the floor for the phera is already hard for you, choose a softer silk that folds easily rather than a stiff one.

For the phera, most mothers still want a pure silk with a proper zari border, and that is right. Just keep the heaviest saree for the phera alone, and pick lighter sarees for the other functions.

Drape and pallu pinning that holds all night

A good drape does more for comfort than any fabric. These small steps help a saree stay in place for a long night.

  1. Pin the pleats in two places. One safety pin at the top of the pleats and one a little lower, both hidden inside. The pleats stay together when you walk and sit.
  2. Pin the pallu at the shoulder. A pinned pallu, with neat folds fixed to the blouse at the shoulder, is easier than an open pallu when you are greeting people and holding plates. Use a brooch on top if you like, with a safety pin underneath doing the real work.
  3. Add one hidden pin at the waist. Pin the pallu's inner edge to the petticoat at the back of the waist. It stops the whole drape from shifting when you bend for the kanyadaan.
  4. Check the hem in the shoes you will wear. Drape the saree wearing your wedding footwear. The hem should just clear the floor so you do not trip on stairs or the stage.
  5. Keep the pallu end tucked near the fire. During the phera, fold or tuck the loose end of the pallu so it stays well away from the havan.

Also make sure the petticoat fits firmly at the waist. A loose petticoat is the most common reason a saree slips late at night.

Colours that look right next to the bride

Most of the wedding album will have the mother standing beside the bride. The goal is simple: look rich and festive, but do not match her. A colour one or two shades away from the bride's outfit, or in a different family altogether, looks best in photos.

Two more photo tips. Under bright stage lights and camera flash, a very shiny all-gold tissue saree can flatten in photos, while a solid colour with a gold border keeps its colour. And if you are unsure which shade suits you, read our guide on how to choose a saree colour for your skin tone.

How many sarees does the bride's mother need?

It depends on your family's functions, but plan one saree for each function you will host or attend. A typical plan looks like this:

  • Haldi or a day function: something light and washable. Our Rivaz weightless silk saree in mustard yellow suits a morning haldi.
  • Bhaat and ladies sangeet: a festive saree with a good border, but not your heaviest. Our Mastani Silk Jerken saree, with gold zari buttis and a wide woven border, comes in Firozi, Rani, Green, Red and Yellow.
  • Phera: your richest pure silk. Our Dulhan HO pure silk saree has a zari border and pallu, and comes in Red, Rani, Yellow and Green. If your daughter is in red, Rani or Green is a good choice for you.
  • Reception: a deep colour that looks good in evening light, or repeat a saree from an earlier function with a different blouse and jewellery.

The bride's mother's bag: a small checklist

  • Ten or twelve safety pins in two sizes
  • A shawl or stole that goes with the phera saree
  • Comfortable footwear for the late hours, if the wedding shoes pinch
  • A spare blouse hook and a small needle and thread
  • A small towel or tissue for the havan smoke and heat

When to buy

Buy the mother's sarees together with the bride's outfit, or soon after, so you can hold them side by side and check the colours. Then give the blouses to the tailor early, because in wedding season tailors are booked out. A common mistake is to buy the mother's sarees last and then run out of time for a proper blouse fitting. Leave yourself two to three weeks for stitching.

Frequently asked questions

What colour saree should the mother of the bride wear?

Choose a rich colour one or two shades away from the bride's outfit. If the bride wears red, rani, deep green, royal blue or mustard with a gold border look good beside her in photos. Avoid matching her exact colour.

Should the mother of the bride wear a heavy silk saree?

Keep your richest pure silk for the phera and kanyadaan, and choose lighter sarees for the other functions. Before buying, lay the pallu over your shoulder for a minute to judge how it will feel after many hours.

How do I stop my saree from slipping during a long wedding?

Pin the pleats in two places, pin the pallu at the shoulder, and add one hidden pin at the back of the waist. A petticoat that fits firmly at the waist also makes a big difference.

Can the mother of the bride wear red?

Many families leave red to the bride so she stands out. If your family custom allows it, choose a red that is clearly different from the bride's, such as a red with a contrast border, or pick rani instead.

How many sarees does the mother of the bride need?

Usually one saree for each function she hosts or attends, often three to five. Sarees from smaller functions can be repeated at the reception with a different blouse and jewellery.

When should the mother of the bride buy her sarees?

Buy them together with the bride's outfit or soon after, so the colours can be checked side by side. Leave two to three weeks for blouse stitching, as tailors are busy in wedding season.
