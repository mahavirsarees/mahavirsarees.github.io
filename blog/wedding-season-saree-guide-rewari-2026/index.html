Wedding Season 2026: How to Choose Your Saree in Rewari

4 November 2026·Mahavir Sarees, Rewari

Short answerWedding season 2026 begins around 21 November (confirm dates with your pandit). Choose the saree first, then book the tailor, because blouses take time in the season. Pick the fabric and colour for each function (haldi, mehendi, sangeet, phera, reception) and for your role in the wedding.

Wedding season in North India usually picks up after Dev Uthani Ekadashi, and this year the first big muhurats begin around 21 November 2026. Please confirm exact dates for your own family with your pandit or the panchang. If you have a wedding in your family, in your street or among your friends, you probably have several functions to dress for. Most families decide the saree late, and then run from shop to shop one week before the wedding. This guide helps you plan early and choose well, whether you are the bride's family, the groom's family, or a guest in Rewari.

Start early: why the saree comes first

A saree is only half the outfit. The blouse has to be stitched and fitted, and during wedding season every tailor in the city is busy. Falls and picos also need to be done on the saree if you want them. So choose your saree first, then book the tailor, and only then think about jewellery and footwear. Buying early also means you can see the saree in daylight at home and change your mind if needed.

A simple plan: list every function you must attend, decide which ones need a new saree and which can repeat something you already own, then buy all the new ones together. Buying together also makes it easier to ask for wholesale rates if you need many pieces for relatives.

Which saree for which function?

There are no strict rules, and every family has its own custom. These are common choices that work well in Haryana weddings.

Haldi and tilak

Yellow is the traditional colour of haldi, and it looks fresh in daylight. A mustard-yellow saree with a printed design, like our Rivaz, is an easy daytime pick. Choose something you do not mind getting a little turmeric on.

Mehendi

Green and other lively colours are popular, and the day is long and relaxed. Choose a saree that is easy to sit in for hours, and keep the pallu simple so it does not get in the way of the mehendi.

Sangeet and cocktail evening

You may be dancing, so comfort matters more than weight. A saree that falls well and does not feel heavy on the shoulder is better than the heaviest one in the shop. Rani, firozi and deep green look good under evening lights.

Phera and the wedding day

Red, rani, maroon and deep green are the classic wedding colours. This is the day for your richest silk, with a good border and pallu. If you are the bride, check that your saree, blouse and dupatta (if any) are all ready at least a few days before the wedding.

Reception

Receptions are usually in the evening, in photos and flash. Rich colours such as teal, bottle green, deep blue and wine look striking. Keep the saree and jewellery balanced: if the saree has a heavy border, choose lighter jewellery.

Choose by your role in the wedding

  • Bride: Traditional red, rani or maroon is the usual choice. Our Dulhan HO Pure Silk saree is made for weddings, bridal functions and festive occasions, and comes in red, rani, yellow and green.
  • Mother or mother-in-law of the bride or groom: You will be on your feet and meeting guests all day. Choose a dignified colour and a saree that is comfortable to carry for many hours.
  • Sister, bhabhi or close relative: You will be in many photos, so choose a colour that stands out from the bride but does not compete with her. Avoid copying the bride's colour.
  • Guest: Wear something rich but not bridal. Skip full bridal red unless you know the family is happy with it.

Fabric and weight: think about the whole day

A wedding saree may stay on for eight to ten hours. In November and December, evenings in Haryana can turn cool, so plan a shawl, and think about whether the saree will be comfortable indoors and outdoors. When you shop, do not only look at it on the table. Hold the saree in your hand, drape the pallu over your arm and feel the weight. A saree that feels heavy in the shop will feel heavier at midnight.

We carry sarees in different fabrics, such as HO Pure Silk, Weightless Silk and Silk Jerken, so you can compare them side by side. If you are not sure which fabric suits a function, ask us to show you a few so you can compare them.

A simple checklist before you buy

  1. Look at the colour in daylight. Shop lights make colours look different. Walk to the door and look again.
  2. Check the border and pallu. Run your hand along the border. It should be even and firm, with no loose threads.
  3. Open the full drape. Look at the design from the shoulder to the pallu, not just the corner on the pile.
  4. Ask about the blouse piece. Check that there is a blouse piece, and what colour it is, before you pay.
  5. Match the saree with your jewellery. Gold jewellery suits most wedding sarees. If you wear silver or polki, carry a small piece of the saree or a photo while you choose.
  6. Keep your tailor's date in mind. Ask your tailor how many days the blouse will take and work backwards from the function.
