What to Wear for Wedding Functions: Haldi to Reception

24 November 2026·Mahavir Sarees, Rewari

Short answerWhat to wear for wedding functions depends on three things: the time of day, how much you will sit or dance, and how close you are to the bride. Wear yellow or a light printed silk for haldi, bright greens and pinks for mehendi, an easy-to-move saree or a lehenga for the sangeet, your richest silk for the phera, and deep jewel colours for the reception. Leave red and heavy zari to the bride.

When a wedding card arrives, the first question in most homes is not "what gift?" but "what to wear for each wedding function?" A Haryanvi wedding can mean five or six functions over three or four days, from a morning haldi in the courtyard to a reception in a banquet hall. Each one asks for a different look. This guide from Mahavir Sarees, Rewari, puts the whole answer in one table: function, colour, fabric, and whether a saree or a lehenga works better. Read the table first, then the notes below it.

The one-table answer: function by function

Every family has its own customs, so treat this as a starting point, not a rule book. It is based on what families from Rewari and nearby villages usually ask for at the counter at Mahavir Sarees, Rewari.

Haldi: easy, yellow and washable

Haldi is the most relaxed function and the messiest. Turmeric stains are hard to remove from good silk, so this is not the day for your best saree. Yellow and mustard are the classic colours, and they look fresh in morning sun.

A printed weightless silk is a practical pick: light, easy to carry through a long morning, and easy to clean. Our Rivaz weightless silk saree in mustard yellow with red and teal arch prints is made for exactly this kind of daytime function.

Mehendi: comfort for long sitting

At mehendi, the bride sits for hours and so do many of the women around her. Choose something you can sit cross-legged in without fuss, with a pallu that will not drag into wet mehendi. Greens, rani pink and bright multi-colour prints all suit the mood.

This is where a lehenga makes sense for many girls, since there is no pallu to manage. For a saree, keep it light. Our Silsila weightless silk saree, with its cream body, red and green patola-style motifs and green border, suits a day mehendi well.

Ladies sangeet: can you dance in it?

Ask one question before you choose: will you be dancing? If yes, a lehenga or a light, well-pinned saree is better than a heavy silk. Heavy borders pull at the shoulder after an hour of dancing. Rani, firozi and emerald look good under evening lights.

A Silk Jerken saree gives a festive look without the weight of full brocade. Our Mastani Silk Jerken saree has small gold zari buttis and a wide woven border, and it is a good sangeet or guest choice.

Bhaat and the baraat welcome: dress for photos

In many Haryana families, the bhaat, when the bride's maternal uncle's family arrives, is a big moment for the bride's mother and her side. It is full of photos, so choose a bright festive colour with a clear border that shows up on camera. The baraat welcome and jaimala are similar: lots of people, lots of photos, and a lot of standing.

Phera: your richest silk, but not the bride's colour

The phera is the main ceremony, and it is the day for your richest silk. For family members, maroon, rani, deep green and mustard all look right next to the havan fire. Leave pure red to the bride unless your family custom says otherwise.

Weddings in our area often have late-night pheras, and in winter that means cold. A dense pure silk is warmer than a thin georgette. Our Dulhan HO pure silk saree, with its zari border and pallu, is the kind of saree we suggest for the phera. For the full winter picture, read our guide to winter wedding saree fabrics.

Reception: deep colours, balanced jewellery

Receptions are usually in the evening, under bright lights and camera flash. Deep jewel tones such as bottle green, navy, wine and teal look striking. If your saree has a heavy border, keep the jewellery lighter, and the other way round. A velvet blouse adds warmth and richness on a winter night.

Saree or lehenga: a quick rule

  • Choose a saree if you are the bride's mother, an aunt or an elder; if you will be greeting guests; or if the function is formal, like the phera or bhaat.
  • Choose a lehenga if you are young and will be dancing, running errands or sitting on the floor for hours, as at mehendi and sangeet. For sisters and cousins, see our guide on lehenga and laccha ideas for the bride's sister.
  • For one function only, pick what you will be most comfortable in for the whole night, not just for the first photo.

Your role decides how bold to go

The bride should stand out in every photo. Close family can wear rich colours but should avoid matching the bride. Guests can be festive, but red with heavy zari is best avoided unless you know the family is fine with it. For role-by-role advice, see our wedding season saree guide for Rewari, and to pick the right shade for you, read how to choose a saree colour for your skin tone.

Checklist before the wedding week

  1. Write down every function you will attend, with the time and whether it is indoors or outdoors.
  2. Decide saree or lehenga for each, using the table above.
  3. Mark which outfits are new and which you can repeat from your cupboard.
  4. Buy all new sarees together, so the colours do not clash across functions.
  5. Give blouses to the tailor early; in wedding season tailors are fully booked.
  6. Keep a shawl and safety pins ready for the late-night functions.

Frequently asked questions

What should I wear to a haldi function?

Yellow, mustard or marigold orange in a light, washable fabric such as printed weightless silk or cotton silk. Turmeric stains are hard to remove, so avoid your best silk saree.

Is a saree or a lehenga better for a sangeet?

If you will be dancing, a lehenga or a light, well-pinned saree is better. A heavy silk saree with a thick border becomes tiring after an hour of dancing.

What colour should family members wear for the phera?

Maroon, rani, deep green and mustard in a rich silk look right at the phera. Leave pure red to the bride unless your family custom says otherwise.

Can guests wear red to an Indian wedding?

It is best to avoid bridal red with heavy zari as a guest. Deep pink, teal, wine or bottle green look just as festive without competing with the bride.

How many outfits do I need for a Haryanvi wedding?

Close family usually needs one outfit for each function they attend, often four to six. Guests attending only the wedding night and reception can manage with one or two.
